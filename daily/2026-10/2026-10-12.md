### 1. Cancerous Backdoor Attack: Gradual Activation via Invisible Tokens and A Stage Controller

📄 [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12644)　📅 2026-10

**关键词**：`attack`、`gradual backdoor`、`invisible tokens`、`stage controller`

👤 **作者**：Tahmid Imtiaz Imu、…、Honghui Xu

- 🎯 **研究动机**：LLM 后门均为触发-终端行为的单点激活——恶意行为能否经多中间阶段渐进发展未被探索
- 🔬 **研究方法**：Cancerous Backdoor：不可见 token 提供隐藏阶段+推理期阶段控制器跟踪攻击进度实现渐进激活
- 📌 **结论**：跨模型跨任务的渐进推理腐蚀——后门激活的时间维度扩展

<details>
<summary>📝 展开完整英文摘要（Abstract）</summary>

Backdoor Attacks on Large Language Models (LLMs) typically follow a trigger-response pattern, where satisfying a trigger condition activates a single terminal behavior. However, this single-point backdoor activation does not explore attacks in which malicious behavior develops gradually through multiple intermediate stages before reaching the terminal activation stage. We present Cancerous Backdoor Attack, that gradually corrupts an LLM's reasoning over a sequence of interactions by leveraging invisible tokens and a stage controller. Specifically, invisible tokens provide hidden stages to the model without appearing in the user's visible request and the stage controller tracks the attack's progression during inference to realize gradual activation. We evaluate Cancerous Backdoor Attack across several reasoning datasets. The attack consistently produces gradual reasoning corruption across models and tasks. We compare Cancerous Backdoor Attack with prior backdoor attack baselines to demonstrate our proposed attack outperforms the baselines. Our results indicate that reasoning corruption can develop gradually before it becomes visible, making answer accuracy alone insufficient for detecting backdoor attacks.

### 2. VEPA: Verified Evolving Formal Policy for Lifelong LLM Agent Guardrails

📄 [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12813)　📅 2026-10

**关键词**：`defense`、`evolving formal policy`、`lifelong guardrail`、`verified adaptation`

👤 **作者**：Duy Nguyen、…、Long T. Le

- 🎯 **研究动机**：运行时 guardrail 需在不可逆动作前做可检查可复现确定性裁决，但安全需求部署前不完备——适应式守门靠 LLM 推理致不一致
- 🔬 **研究方法**：VEPA：guardrail 表示为形式化策略+经验驱动演化经验证合入（每次演化保持可检查与确定性执行）
- 📌 **结论**：终身 agent 守门的形式化验证演化（自适应与确定性的联合）

<details>
<summary>📝 展开完整英文摘要（Abstract）</summary>

LLM agents increasingly operate over sensitive resources, such as private documents and mutable system states, which can create serious safety and security risks. Runtime guardrails should therefore make inspectable, reproducible, and deterministic allow or refuse decisions before potentially irreversible agent actions are executed. However, safety and security requirements are rarely complete before deployment, so guardrails need to keep adapting as new risks emerge. Existing adaptive guardrails learn from deployment experience, but they usually apply safety policies through LLM inference, which can lead to inconsistent decisions and ambiguous policy interpretations. Formal guardrails, on the other hand, enforce policies deterministically, but they usually rely on pre-deployment or task-specific policies and have no mechanism to adapt to newly observed risks. We introduce VEPA, a framework for the verified evolution of formal agent guardrails. VEPA represents the guardrail as a formal policy of executable symbolic rules over typed agent events. During deployment, we use an LLM guided by deployment feedback to propose policy patches that fix newly observed failures while preserving previously correct behavior. Then, a deterministic verifier only accepts policies that are structurally valid and conflict-free. Finally, an evidence-aware authority layer activates a verified rule only when it has enough supporting evidence, so uncertain updates cannot directly affect guardrail decisions. Across ASSEBench-Safety, ASSEBench-Security, and ATBench, VEPA consistently outperforms baselines while expanding the scope of deterministic formal-policy enforcement. These gains also hold under more challenging deployment conditions, including mixed streams, corrupted feedback, and longer horizons.

- 📌 **结论**：经代码与共享记忆传播、跨角色经自然语言规划传播，小开源模型可诱导更强闭源目标——传染性 reward hacking

<details>
<summary>📝 展开完整英文摘要（Abstract）</summary>

Multi-agent systems are increasingly used to solve coding tasks by coordinating agents that share code and plans. These agents are often optimized with reinforcement learning (RL) using automated evaluators, but bypassing evaluation can earn reward without producing a genuine solution. Prior work has largely treated reward hacking as a single-agent failure. In this work, we show that a reward-hacking strategy elicited in one agent can propagate to others and affect system coordination. Across four open-weight backbones, coding RL elicits and amplifies reward hacking in two settings: explicit mechanism descriptions in the system prompt and mid-training that models upstream exposure to facts about the coding environment. Using post-RL agents from the mid-training setting as sources, we observe propagation through code and shared memory, and across roles through natural-language plans. We also theoretically analyze conditions under which reward hacking amplifies, persists, or decays across agent interactions. Beyond propagation, reward hacking can bias task routing toward reward-hacking agents and survive candidate aggregation. As potential mitigations, we evaluate code review by the coder or a separate verifier, both of which reduce delivered hacks. Together, these results establish reward hacking as a transmissible strategy whose effects extend beyond the agent in which it first emerges.

### 5. Understanding and Mitigating Cross-Session Attacks via Persistent Artifacts in Agent Skill Composition

📄 [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13227)　📅 2026-10

**关键词**：`attack`、`cross-session attack`、`persistent artifacts`、`skill composition`

👤 **作者**：Dianshu Liao、Shidong Pan、Guangdong Bai、Zhenchang Xing、Xiaoyu Sun

- 🎯 **研究动机**：skill 执行产生/修改的工件（文件/配置/缓存）跨会话被其他 skill 复用——新攻击面未被探索
- 🔬 **研究方法**：三种跨会话攻击机制：指令传播（IP）/数据边界违反（DBV）/证据投毒（EP）+运行时防御
- 📌 **结论**：持久工件成为恶意效果跨 skill 执行传播的通道（skill 组合供应链的时序扩展）

<details>
<summary>📝 展开完整英文摘要（Abstract）</summary>

Agent skills extend the capabilities of AI agents by bundling instructions, code, and resources into reusable packages. During skill execution, agents may produce or modify artifacts, such as files, configurations, and cached data, that are reused by other skills in later sessions. Since these artifacts can persist across sessions and later be accessed by other skills, they introduce a new attack surface that can be exploited to influence subsequent executions. However, the security risks arising from such cross-session skill interactions remain largely unexplored. In this work, we investigate cross-session composition attacks in skill-based AI agents and develop a runtime defense against them. Specifically, we characterize three attack mechanisms, namely Instruction Propagation (IP), Data Boundary Violation (DBV), and Evidence Poisoning (EP), through which malicious effects propagate across skill executions via persistent artifacts. Successful attack chains cover all five target security consequence categories, highlighting the broad impact of these attacks. To mitigate these risks, we propose SkillRelayGuard, which restores artifact provenance context and provides pre-execution advisory review while leaving the final execution decision to the agent. Evaluation across three agent settings shows that SkillRelayGuard reduces overall ASR from 69.5% to 10.9% and ACSR from 54.8% to 10.0%, while increasing legitimate task completion from 79.6% to 87.5%.

<details>
<summary>📝 展开完整英文摘要（Abstract）</summary>

Large language model (LLM) agents are highly vulnerable to memory-poisoning attacks. Existing defenses primarily rely on external modules for either static memory isolation or continuous online auditing, resulting in low memory efficiency and high computational overhead. To address these limitations, inspired by neuroscientific mechanisms that weak reactivation can destabilize memories and induce decay in the absence of reinforcement, we propose an agent-intrinsic training-free memory repair framework, DREAM (Dynamic Reactivation for Engram Attenuation in Memory), that enables selective functional forgetting of poisoned memories. Extensive experiments across diverse attackers and real-world tasks demonstrate that DREAM reduces attack success rates by over 95\% against backdoor poisoning while preserving strong benign utility. DREAM also achieves competitive robustness against injection attacks. In terms of runtime and token consumption, DREAM achieves up to a 2.13$\times$ speedup and a 37.7\% reduction in token consumption compared with A-MemGuard. Furthermore, DREAM also achieves a task success rate of 92.96\% on poisoned multi-agent systems.

A central challenge in embodied recursive self-improvement is enabling agents to convert interaction history into capabilities that persist beyond one world and transfer to later tasks. A saved memory or successful final action does not by itself establish that a reusable capability was acquired; evaluation must separate capability change from carried-over resources, unequal source exposure, and early execution failures. We construct MC-EvoBench as a controlled Minecraft benchmark with a fixed catalog of 19 registered evolution chains, including 12 primary evaluation chains and seven calibration or probe chains, together with three graph-aligned paired probe families for transfer. The benchmark represents each task as a task-state graph, defines stage-level environment verifiers, assigns matched source and target roles, resets the target world, and permits only a declared agent artifact to cross the source-target boundary. For each probe, no-prior, related, unrelated, and shuffled source conditions are evaluated under matched environment-step budgets and five seeds; we report TSR, WGP, and VMP and retain provenance records. The benchmark reveals a consistent direct-capability gap between controller and planner-backed variants, while related experience yields no stable transfer: it does not improve either tool probe, its small wood-species gain hinges on one seed, and all intervals include zero. Stage audits further show that early wood-collection and crafting bottlenecks often leave later capabilities unobserved.
